# Apple Financial Analysis

## Revenue

### Observation 

- Revenue groth was mostly driven by the services segment. 
- Product growth has been mostly flat.

### Evidence

- Revenue grew (2.80%) from FY2022-23, 2.02% from FY2023-24, and 6.43% from FY2024-25. 
- Total revenue growth since FY2023 = 5.65%
- Product revenue growth from FY2022-23 = (5.73%), from FY2023-24 = (1.08%), from FY2024-25 = 4.12%
- Service revenue growth from FY2022-23 = 9.05%, from FY2023-24 = 12.87%, from FY2024-25 = 13.52%
- Products as % of revenue fell from 77.77% in FY2023 to 73.77% in FY2025
- Services as a % of revenue increased from 22.23% in FY2023 to 26.23% in FY2025. 
        
### Explaination

- Revenue in the hardware sector is driven by the increase in installed bases and upgraders. The growth seen in FY2025 is a result of increased demand due to product innovation. The iPhone 16 and 17 family proved to be a stronger lineup as it saw increase in upgraders by a double digit % from the iPhone 15 lineup. Mac and iPad also recorded increase in installed base by almost a half and a double digit % increase in upgraders. 
- The service sector is been consistently recording increase in viewership, sale on Apple store, and year-over-year growing iCloud paying accounts, which was also influenced by the online launch in Saudi Arabia. 
- Product growth in FY2025 was also a factor of new organisations deploying Macs and iPads for their organisations.

### Investor Importance

- Revenue growth seems to be highly driven by growth in the service segment. This is reducing dependency on sale of hardware to increase margins. 
- Apple increased CapEx by 34.60% in FY2025 which was associated with building out the private cloud compute environment in Apple’s first-party data centers. This could show results in better AI capability, enhancing the user experience and imporving the ecosystem standards. Apple's ecosystem moat would strenghten leading to customer retention due to increased switching costs that would generate higher service margins in future. 
- Apple's growing installed base and upgraders supports a growing services ecosystem, increasing margins and reccuring revenue. 

## Gross Margin

### Observation 

- Apple's gross margin increased from 44.13% to 46.91% between FY2023 and FY2025, driven primarily by expansion in Services margins while Product margins remained broadly stable.

### Evidence 

- The gross margin was recorded 44.13% in FY2023, 46.21% in FY2024, and 46.91% in FY2025.
- The products margin increased from 36.50% to 37.20% between FY2023-24, and declined to 36.80% in FY2025
- The service margin was recorded 70.80% in FY2023, 73.90% in FY2024, and 75.40% in FY2025.
- The service segment generated 42.16% of Apple's total gross profit in FY2025, 39.32% in FY2024, and 35.68% in FY2023
- The product segment generated 57.84% of Apple's total gross profit in FY2025, 60.68% in FY2024, and 64.32% in FY2023. 
  
### Explaination 

- The service segment contribution has seen a steady increase since FY2023 as a result of Apple's installed base and continued engagement with the ecosystem.
- There has been a double digit year-over-year growth in subscriptions.
- Apple has also incurred $800 million in tariffs in Q2 which also affected the profitability of products and the primary reason of gross margin being down 60 basis points from Q1 FY2025 to Q2 FY2025. 
- Services gross margin increased by 4.6 percentage points from FY2023 to FY2025 while product margin stayed stable. 

### Investor Importance

- Increase in the service margin improves Apple's earning quality and profitability.
- Tariffs represent the material cost pressure in FY2025 Q2 directly affecting the profitability.
- Rise in installed bases as well as upgraders indicated that Apple has had a continued demnad for it's products, although it's sustainability depends on customer engagement and competitive product innovation.
- Apple however cannot continue to keep expanding its service margin and shift focus from the product segment as the product segment is the primary driver of increasing installed bases that generates service margins. 

## Operating Margin

## Observation 

- The operating margin remained fairly stable from FY2023-24 before expanding significantly in FY2025, while operating costs as a percentage of revenue decresed.  

## Evidence 

- Operating margin recorded in FY2025 is 58.96%, 55.41% in FY2024, 55.28% in FY2023.
- R&D expenses recorded 7.80% of sales in FY2023, 8.02% of sales in FY2024, and 8.30% of sales in FY2025.
- SG&A expenses were recorded 6.50% of sales in FY2023, 6.67% of sales in FY2024, and 6.63% of sales in FY2025.

### Explaination 

- The cost of products of sales declined from 49.38% of sales in FY2023 to 46.64% of sales in FY2025. The R&D expenses showed a change of 0.5 percentage points, mainly attributed towards investment in AI and their efforts to go carbon neutral by 2030 as stated by the management. 
- As mentioned by the management, every new product they launch has a more expensive cost structure than it replaces due to additional technology, that drives up the cost of product segments and puts pressure on the cost margins.
- The Q4 FY2025 earning call clearly mentions that increase is operating expenses is driven by investment in R&D. The R&D expenses rose 4.86% from FY2023 to FY2024, and 10.13% from FY2024 to FY2025.
- The increase in SG&A expenses was primararily driven by head-count related expenses and variable selling expenses as recorded in Form 10-K of FY2025.

### Investor Importance 

- The management mentioned in the Q4 FY 2023 earnings call that some of the investments that they're making in R&D are one of the drivers of their gross margins expanding. Apple expanded operating margin despite increasing R&D intensity, because the improvement in gross profitability was not fully absorbed by operating expenses.
- Continued R&D investment may support future product and services innovation, but the investment will need to generate sufficient incremental revenue or margin improvement to justify the rising expenditure.
- Apple's operating margin expansion was a result of gross margin rising, particularly due to services which more than offset the increasing investment in R&D, while the SG&A expenses remained stable as a % of revenue. 

## Net Profit Margin 

### Observation 

- Net profit margin declined by 1.34 percentage points between FY2023 and FY2024 before recovering by 2.95 percentage points in FY2025, broadly coinciding with a significant increase and subsequent decline in income-tax expense as a proportion of revenue.

### Evidence 

- The NPM in FY2023 was 25.31%, in FY2024 was 23.97%, and in FY2025 was 26.92%.
- The provision for income tax as a % of revenue was 4.37% in FY2023, 7.61% in FY2024, and 4.98% in FY2025.
- The effective tax rate in FY2025 was 15.6%, 24.1% in FY2024, and 14.7% in FY2023.

### Explaination 

- The 9.4 percentage points spike in 2024 followed the Europeon Court of Justice ruling which resulted in a one-time income tax charge of $10.2b, net, which represented $15.8b payable to Ireland via release of amounts held in escrow, partially offset by a US foreign tax credit of $4.8b and a decrease in unrecognisable tax benefits of $823m, during the fourth quater of 2024.
- The NPM recovery in FY2025 was supported by the expansion of the operating margin and reduced tax burden compared to the previous year.

### Investor Importance 

- FY2024's NPM decline was not a result of operating performace but a one-time tax charge.
- FY2025's recovery demonstrates stronger earnings conversion, as the operating margin expands and the effective tax rate falls.
- Therefore the key risk is regulatory changes that can create volatility in net earnings that can temporarily offset increasing margins and operating performace. 

## Cash Flow

### Observation 

- The cashflow from operating activites declined 5.76% in FY2025, despite increase in net income while free cash flow declined from its peak in FY2024 but maintained a similar level to FY2023. Cash used in financing activities increased 12.43% in FY2024 and remained broadly stable in FY2025. Cash generated from investing activites increased 417.71% in FY2025 as comapred to the 20.78% decline in FY2024 primarily reflecting lower purchases of marketable securities, while financing cash outflows remained broadly stable.

### Evidence   

- The net income generated was $112.010b in FY2025, $93.736b in FY2024, and $96.995b in FY2023.
- Cash generated by operating activities was $111.482b in FY2025, $118.254b in FY2024, and $110.543b in FY2023.
- Cash generated by investing activities was $15.195b in FY2025, $2.935b in FY2024, and $3.705b in FY2023.
- Cash used in financing activities was $120.686b in FY2025, $121.983b in FY2024, and $108.488b in FY2023.
- Operating cash flow as a percentage of net income was recorded 99.53% in FY2025, 126.16% in Fy2024, and 113.97% in FY2023.
- Free cash flow was recorded at $98.767b in FY2025, $108.807b in FY2024, and $99.584b in FY2023.
- Net income increased 19.51% from FY2024 to FY2025, while operating cash flow declined 5.72%.
- FCF declined 9.23% from FY2024 to FY2025, from $108.807bn to $98.767bn. 

### Explaination

- The operating cash flow fell below net income in FY2025 as chnages in operating assets and liabilities reduced cash generating by $25b, largely offesetting the $24.427b non cash adejustments.  
- In FY2024 the operating assets and liabilities movement contributed $3.651b to the net income along with $20.867b as non-cash adjustments that supported the cash generated from operations.
- The cash generated from investing activities increased in FY2025 primarily due to a reduction in purchase of marketable securities from $48.656b in FY2024 to $24.407b, while the payments for property, plan, and equipment increased from $9.447b in FY2024 to $12.715b in FY2025.   
- FY2024 investing cash flow was lower because purchases of marketable securities increased to $48.656bn from $29.513bn in FY2023.
- Financing cash outflows remained broadly stable in FY2025, with share repurchases representing a major use of cash at $90.711b, compared with $94.949bn in FY2024 and $77.550bn in FY2023. The increase in repurchases was a major contributor to the higher financing cash outflow in FY2024.
- FY2024 generated the highest FCF because operating cash flow reached $118.254bn while CapEx fell to $9.447bn. FY2025 FCF declined to $98.767bn as operating cash flow fell and CapEx increased to $12.715bn.

### Investor Importance

- Apple's ability to generate approximately $99bn of FCF in FY2025 demonstrates substantial internal cash-generation capacity despite higher CapEx and lower operating cash flow.
- The increase in FY2025 CapEx indicates that Apple can increase investment while continuing to generate substantial FCF, although the financial return from these investments remains uncertain.
- The divergence between net income and operating cash flow in FY2025 means investors should monitor cash conversion alongside earnings growth when assessing the sustainability of Apple's financial performance.

## Capital Allocation

### Observation 

- Apple consistently returns substantial capital to shareholders through share repurchases and dividends while maintaining significant investment in capital expenditure. FY2025 saw higher CapEx and lower operating cash flow, resulting in lower free cash flow available for capital allocation. 

### Evidence 

- Apple repurchsed shares every year from 2023-25. $90.711b in FY2025, $94.949b in FY2024, and $77.550b in FY2023.
- Apple paid $15.421b in dividends in FY2025, $15.234b in FY2024, $15.025b in FY2023.
- The CapEX was $12.715b in FY2025, $9.447b in FY2024, and $10.959b in FY2023.
- Free cash flow was recorded at $98.767b in FY2025, $108.807b in FY2024, and $99.584b in FY2023.
- The financing cashflow was $120.686b in FY2025, $121.983b in FY2024, $108.488b in FY2023.
- Cash generated by operating activities was $111.482b in FY2025, $118.254b in FY2024, and $110.543b in FY2023.

### Explaination 

- Shareholder returns exceeded FCF in FY2024 and FY2025, while Apple's cash and marketable securities declined over the period, indicating that distributions were supported not only by current-period FCF but also by Apple's existing liquidity.
- Total term debt declined from $105.103bn in FY2023 to $90.678bn in FY2025, while CapEx increased in FY2025.
- The 5.05% reduction in basic shares and 5.11% reduction in diluted shares indicate that sustained repurchases have materially reduced the share base, providing mechanical support to EPS growth, all else equal.
- Diluted EPS increased by $1.33, from $6.13 in FY2023 to $7.46 in FY2025, supported by both higher net income and the reduction in diluted shares.
- Despite distributions exceeding FCF in FY2024 and FY2025, total term debt also declined, indicating that Apple was not relying primarily on increasing debt to fund shareholder returns.
   
### Investor Importance 

- Shareholder distributions exceeding FCF while liquid assets decline means the sustainability of the current distribution rate deserves monitoring.
- EPS growth was supported by both higher net income and a lower share count. The increase in net profit margin indicates that EPS growth was not solely dependent on share repurchases.
  
## Balance Sheet

### Observation 

- Apple maintains limited short-term liquidity while remaining in a net cash position. Despite substantial shareholder distributions, leverage declined and shareholders' equity recovered in FY2025.

### Evidence 

- The net cash figure stands at $41.742b in FY2025, $59.988b in FY2024, and $56.996b in FY2023.
- The total term debt is $90.678b in FY2025, $96.662b in FY2024, and $105.103b in FY2023.
- The current ratio for FY2025 was 0.89x, FY2024 was 0.87x, and FY2023 was 0.99x.
- The quick ratio for FY2025 was 0.86x, FY2024 was 0.83x, and FY2023 was 0.94x.
- The total debt to equity ratio was 1.23x in FY2025, 1.70x in FY2024, and 1.69x in FY2023.
- The proprietery ratio was 0.21x in FY2025, 0.16x in FY2024, and 0.18x in FY2023.
- The total debt to total assets ratio was 0.25x in FY2025, 0.26x in FY2024, and 0.30 in FY2023.
- The shareholders' equity amounted to $73.733b in FY2025, $56.950b in FY2024, $62.146b in FY2023.
- The working capital was -$17.674b in FY2025, -$23.405b in FY2024, and -$1.742b in FY2023.
- The total assets amount to $359.241b in FY2025, $364.980b in FY2024, and $352.583b in FY2023. 

### Explaination 

- Apple stands at net cash position of $41.742b in FY2025 despite its <1 current ratio and a negative working capital, but it generates $111.482b operational cash flow to support the business instead of relying on debt. The current ratio alone overstates the risk.
- Apple's debt burden reduced 13.72% since FY2023 while its assets remained broadly at the same level as reflected in the decling debt to assets ratio.
- Debt/equity fell because term debt declined while shareholders' equity increased, with FY2025 net income more than offsetting the substantial reduction in equity from share repurchases and dividends.
- Apple has generated significant net cash to cover its returns to shareholder, pay back term debt, finance its operations, and maintain a net cash position.

### Investor Importance

- Apple's negative working capital and sub-1x current ratio create reliance on continued operating cash generation and access to liquid investments to meet short-term obligations. This risk is mitigated by its $41.7bn net cash position and $111.5bn of FY2025 operating cash flow.
- The decline in term debt and debt-to-assets ratio indicates improving balance-sheet leverage, while the recovery in shareholders' equity provides additional balance-sheet resilience.
- However, the decline in net cash alongside shareholder distributions exceeding FCF should be monitored to assess whether Apple's current capital-return rate remains sustainable without materially reducing financial flexibility. 
 
## Overall Financial Health

### Thesis

Apple's financial position is strong because profitability, cash generation, and leverage remain strong, but its financial flexibility and future earnings growth increasingly depend on maintaining hardware competitiveness and disciplined capital allocation.

#### Financial Strength

Apple accounted for $112.010b net income in FY2025, and generated $111.482b of operating cash flow out of which $98.767b was free cash flow. Apple's net income was supported by a growing gross margin driven by the expansion of service margin while products susbtituted >70% of the revenue. The operating margin also grew by 3.68 percentage points, despite increasing R&D expense. Apple generated operating cash flow equivalent to 99.53% of net income in FY2025, 126.16% in FY2024, and 113.97% in FY2023, indicating strong cash conversion despite working-capital movements. The FY2025 ratio was lower because operating cash flow declined against net income due to working capital movements. 

#### Quality of Earnings

Operating cash flow remained broadly aligned with or above net income across the period, indicating that reported earnings were generally supported by cash generation rather than being driven primarily by non-cash accounting effects. The growing service margins indicates that service revenue is growing faster than products revenue. The weak NPM in FY2024 was a result of a one-time tax charge as the gross margin showed growth of 2.08 percentage points and not a decline in revenue. The future growth of service margin depends on the growth of the hardware segment to increase the installed base for reccuring revenue. The product revenue, however, grew 4.12% in FY2025 and contributes to more than 70% of the total revenue. 

#### Capital Allocation & Financial Flexibility

Shareholder distributions exceeded FY2025 free cash flow, while Apple's liquid asset position and term debt position declined, indicating that distributions were not fully funded by current-period free cash flow. The company has effectively reduced its basic share count by 5.05% since FY2023. The company also increased its CapEx by 34.60% in FY2025 and maintained a $41.7b net cash position. Apple is increasing shareholder distributions without losing the net cash position. This should be monitored because sustained distributions above free cash flow could gradually reduce financial flexibility if the pattern persists.

#### Key Risks

- Apple's increasing dependence on service margins might weaken the sustainability of growth because the product revenue growth was -1.08% in FY2024 and -5.73% in FY2023. However the 4.12% growth of FY2025 in hardware revenue is a positive but the declining product revenue as a % of total revenue should be monitored. The growth in service margin is highly dependent on product innovation which facilitates the increase in the installed base and upgraders. A sustained slowdown in product sale could constrain the growing installed bases and therefore limit the consumer base which drive future service growth.

- Apple needs sustained innovation to protect its pricing power, ecosystem and margins, while the cost of innovation is rising. The management did state that the growing margins were supported by their increasing focus on R&D, although a continued ROI is uncertain.

- AThe decline in liquid assets and shareholder distributions exceeding free cash flow reduce financial flexibility at the margin. If this pattern persisted alongside weaker operating cash generation, Apple would have less internally generated liquidity available for investment, debt reduction and shareholder returns.    

### Overall Investor Assessment

Apple is financially strong today, but its future financial strength depends on whether it can continue generating sufficient cash to simultaneously fund innovation, maintain hardware competitiveness, support Services growth and return substantial capital to shareholders.
